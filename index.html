Week 3 Recap

Two Teams Remain Unbeaten as Week 3 Delivers Six Different Stories

By the League Newsroom · Week 3 Edition

Three weeks in, and the league is starting to separate into tiers. Justin and Borovay are the only teams left without a blemish, and both are getting it from everywhere — Jaxon Smith-Njigba is up to 405 yards and 6 touchdowns on Justin's side, while Borovay just got Brock Bowers back and watched him look like he never missed a snap. The middle of the standings is a pile-up: six teams sit at 1-2, separated by matchups as much as talent, and a single swing game could rearrange half the league by next week. Kirk Cousins quietly extending his streak to three straight multi-touchdown games and Jahmyr Gibbs putting up another 150-plus-yard, 3-score outburst are the kind of performances that don't show up in a final score but decide who's actually building something.

The injury report also took a beating. De'Von Achane's torn ACL ends his season outright, and the walking wounded list grew fast behind him — Travis Etienne (hamstring), Baker Mayfield (dislocated thumb), Justin Jefferson (ankle), and Breece Hall (thigh) all picked up new ones in Week 3 alone. For a league that already lost Jaxon Dart for the year and Jayden Daniels to a dislocated shoulder, the waiver wire is about to get busy. Full breakdowns below.

Shane Falco Division

The Blue Bloods
Fat JaMarcus Russell Edges Get Griddy With It by Less Than a Point

One of the closest matchups in league history. Justin holds off Nick by less than a point to move to 3-0 for the first time. Jaxon Smith-Njigba was the engine again — 10 catches, 128 yards, 2 touchdowns, now up to 405 yards and 6 scores through three weeks. Kittle looks fully back — 6 catches, 82 yards, 2 touchdowns. Stafford threw for 390 yards and 2 scores. The winning points came Monday night from an unlikely source: Kyle Monangai managed just 31 rushing yards, but it was enough. The one downside — Travis Etienne's hamstring is going to cost him a few weeks, a tough break for a team already thin at running back. Final: Justin.

Nick's first loss comes down to a couple of missed opportunities. Justin Jefferson got hurt early and never returned — one more catch out of him likely flips this game. Sam Darnold made his return and delivered 4 touchdown passes. Josh Allen had his worst outing of the year but still found the end zone twice. Drake London clearly prefers a quarterback not named Cooper Rush — 9 catches, 194 yards. Derrick Henry scored twice more, now up to 6 on the season. But the early Jefferson injury and a quiet week from Tet McMillan were enough to end the unbeaten run.

— Colin Audible
Unsolicited Dak Pics Avoids 0-3, Sends The Hurts Locker There Instead

Parker (1-2) finally breaks through. CMC had his best game of the year — 100-plus yards and a score. Javonte Williams found the end zone again, adding 98 yards on the ground. Christian Watson keeps breaking out — 7 catches, 96 yards, a touchdown, and he's now scored in every game this season. Kyler Murray's return wasn't pretty — 168 yards, 1 score — but the Vikings defense is turning into a real fantasy weapon, 20-plus points in back-to-back weeks. Final: Parker.

Rebecca's first 0-3 start ever, and the roster just can't find its groove. Malik Nabers doesn't have 100 receiving yards total through three weeks and still hasn't found the end zone. Breece Hall's banged up now too, a thigh injury to track. Both Jalen Hurts and Bryce Young turned in their worst games of the year. The one bright spot: Jaylen Warren broke out. Rebecca isn't out of it yet, but this is a must-win spot.

— Colin Audible

Bobbie Miles Division

Ring Row
Baker's Bunch Handles BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

There's a version of this story where Robby has been building toward this moment for weeks, and Sunday is when it finally arrived. Jeremiah Love, a first-round investment that's tested Robby's patience through two quiet weeks, finally broke free for over 100 yards and a score — the kind of afternoon that makes an early pick look like foresight instead of a reach. Harold Fannin found paydirt twice. And then there was Davante Adams, who's turned Puka Nacua's absence into an opportunity nobody wishes on anyone but everybody would take — 15 catches, 332 yards, 2 touchdowns of pure opportunism. Trevor Lawrence and Joe Burrow traded haymakers from the quarterback slot, three scores apiece. But the quietest development might be the loudest going forward: Zay Flowers is healthy again, back in the starting lineup for the first time since his injury, and looked every bit himself — 5 catches for 84 yards in his return. Robby's receiver room, already good, just got its third piece back.

Somewhere in Charlie's building, someone is still trying to explain Kirk Cousins. Three straight games, three touchdown passes each time — the surprise of the season refuses to be a one-week story. But this one belonged to what didn't happen. Cam Ward came back to earth hard, failing to reach double digits after his breakout. Amon-Ra St. Brown, who has carried this roster more weeks than not, turned in his quietest game of the year — 4 catches, 19 yards, saved from disaster only by a late touchdown. Kyren Williams gave Charlie another bright spot, 158 total yards, his best showing yet. Isaiah Likely, meanwhile, is starting to feel like a Week 1 mirage — quiet again, and the questions are piling up.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Jayden's Mambo No. 4s Bounces Back, Downs DAMNNN Daniel!!

Billed as the game of the week, and it lived up to the marketing — right up until Jordan pulled away and made it look easy. The story starts and ends with Bijan Robinson, who put together 200-plus total yards and 2 touchdowns behind an Atlanta offense that looks unrecognizable with Michael Penix under center. Kenneth Walker keeps quietly doing Kenneth Walker things — 70-plus yards, 2 more scores, a third straight week north of 20 points. Rashee Rice is emerging as a real weapon in Kansas City, and there's reinforcements on the way: Nico Collins could be back this week. But keep both eyes on Kenyon Sadiq, who hauled in 7 catches for 105 yards and a score. A new starting tight end, hiding in plain sight?

This one hurt Matt in more ways than the scoreboard shows. Yes, it's a second straight loss. But the news that's going to sting for months is De'Von Achane's torn ACL, a season-ending blow to a running back room that had no cushion to spare. Jalen Coker finally earned his way into the starting lineup, then strained his quad before he could make the most of it. Lost in the wreckage: Brock Purdy quietly threw for 297 yards and 4 touchdowns, now the league's #2 quarterback through three weeks, and Ja'Marr Chase posted his second straight 20-plus point game. Sometimes the box score tells you a team is fine. This one tells you something else entirely.

— Frank Lee Hurtz

Paul Crewe Division

Contenders & Ghosts
Mahomes Depot Stays Perfect, Handles Barking up the Wrong Tree

The numbers on Borovay keep getting sillier. 3-0, his best start since 2019, when he opened that year on a seven-game heater — this is the same trajectory. Tyler Shough is now, statistically, the #3 quarterback in the league through three weeks, which is the kind of sentence that reads like a typo until you check the box score yourself. Four more touchdown passes this week. Brock Bowers made his season debut and produced like a player who'd never missed a game — 10 catches, 116 yards, a touchdown. James Cook is in the middle of his second straight 20-plus point week, 154 yards and a score. Parker Washington adds another touchdown to a pile that's starting to look less like a trend and more like a role. There is very little left to project here — the evidence has arrived.

A new floor for the league's lowest-scoring team, which is its own kind of achievement. Goff (269 yards, 2 touchdowns) and Dak (276 yards, a score) did their jobs — again, that quarterback room simply is not the issue anymore. The issue is everyone else: not a single other starter reached double digits. Chase Brown keeps accumulating touches without ever finding the end zone, which is a fine way to accumulate nothing. But the real headline is Saquon Barkley, who has now gone three games without a double-digit fantasy performance and zero touchdowns to show for it — the kind of drought that stops being a slump and starts being the profile. Tucker Kraft's line — 9 catches, 106 yards, no scores — is the quietest kind of disappointing.

— Ivana Blitz
Breece Yourselves Finally Breaks Through Against The Idea of BO-lieve

The math on Suekoff's season finally moved in the right direction. Garrett Wilson posted his best game of the year — 10 catches, 107 yards, a touchdown — and Bhayshul Tuten is quietly building a case for himself, a second straight game with a score and 90 total yards to go with it. Marcus Mariota threw 3 touchdowns, numbers that look better than the offense around him has any right to produce. That offense is still a concern on paper, and the data just got worse — Baker Mayfield's out with a dislocated thumb, which is not going to help Emeka Egbuka's already-thin production. Tyler Warren's quiet 9-catch, 55-yard game deserves a mention too, buried as it is under everything else.

Same conclusion, different sample size, for Drake Maye — his worst statistical output yet, 3 turnovers on the ledger. Bo Nix at least generated points, if not efficiency — 3 touchdowns on a 50% completion rate, the kind of line that helps the box score and nothing else. Jahmyr Gibbs remains the one sure thing on this roster, another 164 yards and 3 touchdowns. Chris Olave cleared 100 receiving yards, and Trey McBride's 9-catch, 75-yard day came without the touchdown to match. The concerning data point: Jadarian Price's workload keeps shrinking, down to 5 carries, and Jaylen Waddle's 2-catch, 10-yard week is about as far from his ceiling as the numbers get.

— Ivana Blitz

Week 4 Matchups
The schedule opens up — first week out of division play, and everybody draws an opponent from outside their own neighborhood.
Writer's Record: 8-10 on the season (2-4 in Week 3).

Get Griddy With It vs Jayden's Mambo No. 4s

Match of the week. Nick (2-1) and Jordan (2-1) project as the highest-scoring, tightest game on the slate — two loaded rosters, two MVP-caliber quarterbacks. Josh Allen against Lamar Jackson. Derrick Henry against Bijan Robinson. On paper, a heavyweight fight, and maybe a playoff preview.

In practice, it won't be decided by the names at the top. The stars will shine — that much is a given. The real question is who steps up behind them. Jordan needs Kenyon Sadiq and Mike Evans finding room underneath the big plays. Nick needs Sam Darnold staying upright long enough for Tet McMillan to do something with it. Whichever supporting cast shows up wins this game.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Get Griddy With It

Fat JaMarcus Russell vs DAMNNN Daniel!!

There's something almost poetic about perfection meeting desperation. Justin arrives at 3-0, spotless, while Matt limps in off back-to-back losses and an Achane injury that ended a season before it really got going. But spotless doesn't mean comfortable — Caleb Williams remains sidelined, Travis Etienne alongside him, and suddenly a great deal rides on the shoulders that are used to carrying it: Jaxon Smith-Njigba, George Kittle, Matthew Stafford. There isn't much margin left to work with.

Matt, for his part, is waiting on a whisper more than a promise — word is Puka Nacua might suit up this week. If he does, the calculus here shifts hard. Add in Ja'Marr Chase playing like the best receiver in football and Brock Purdy quietly climbing the quarterback rankings, and Matt has more than a puncher's chance. He has, dare it be said, the makings of an upset.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: DAMNNN Daniel!! (upset)

Barking up the Wrong Tree vs Baker's Bunch

The numbers here aren't subtle. Robby put up the highest score in the league last week. Ross put up the lowest. That's less a matchup and more a math problem with the answer already circled.

Robby's got Zay Flowers back in the lineup and Jeremiah Love coming off the best game of his career — Burrow and Lawrence should be more than enough stacked on top of that. Ross needs answers, and fast: Saquon Barkley needs to find the end zone, Chase Brown needs a touchdown to show for his touches, Ladd McConkey needs targets, Tucker Kraft needs literally anything. Start there. Then worry about the rest.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: Baker's Bunch

BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY vs The Idea of BO-lieve

Two teams treading water, both hunting for .500. Gembo's dropped two straight and would love to bench Drake Maye, but the bench doesn't offer much of a rescue plan. Good thing Jahmyr Gibbs is playing like a problem nobody's solved — six touchdowns and counting, with Chris Olave and Trey McBride trailing close behind.

Somewhere, a very confused league keeps waiting for Kirko Chainz to come back to earth. Three touchdown passes a game, and nobody saw it coming. Kyren Williams and Tony Pollard just turned in their best games of the season — but MarShawn Lloyd and Cam Ward aren't holding up their end, and that gap is exactly where this one gets decided.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: The Idea of BO-lieve

Breece Yourselves vs The Hurts Locker

On paper, the quarterback room tilts hard toward Rebecca — Hurts and Young against Jacoby Brissett shouldn't be a fair fight. And yet last week, Brissett outscored the two of them combined. Don't bank on the rerun.

Forget the rest of the roster for a second — the real subplot is a 1-on-1. Emeka Egbuka for Suekoff against Malik Nabers for Rebecca: two talented young receivers stuck catching passes from backup-caliber arms, both waiting for their numbers to catch up to their talent. Whoever puts up more points probably decides this one. (Don't actually make lineup decisions off that. Probably.)

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: The Hurts Locker

Unsolicited Dak Pics vs Mahomes Depot

Records say blowout. Roster says close game. Borovay holds the edge at quarterback, running Mahomes and Shough against Kyler Murray and Jordan Love. Parker holds the edge at running back, but barely — James Cook, Skattebo, and Swift are all playing well for Borovay, it's just that CMC alone is enough to nudge Parker's group over the top. Receivers lean Borovay, CeeDee Lamb and Parker Washington against a Christian Watson-led room that also has to find room for Pickens and Moore. Tight end's a wash.

That leaves the deciding factor somewhere nobody expected: Minnesota's defense against Miami. Back-to-back 20-plus point weeks for that unit, and nothing about Miami suggests that stops now. If it doesn't, Parker hands Borovay his first loss.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Unsolicited Dak Pics
