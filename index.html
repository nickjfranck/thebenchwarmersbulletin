Season Preview

Fourteen Seasons In, the Devils Kick Off Again

By the League Newsroom · Preseason Edition

Fourteen years is a long time to keep people showing up every September. The league started with 10 teams back in 2013, grew to 12 as five new faces joined over the years, and has said goodbye to three of the original ten along the way. Rosters turn over, team names get rebranded, kids get born, and somehow the league survives all of it. Season 14 of the League of Extraordinary Devils kicks off this week with a new name in the record book — Borovay's Mahomes Depot snuck into the playoffs at 7-7 last year and ran the table for his first championship. That leaves exactly one of the seven remaining original franchises still searching for its first title: Gembo. For everyone else, there's a full season to try to knock the new champ back down.

It was a good draft, the dues are all paid, and at this point nobody has any excuse to miss a game — not when football is somehow streaming on a different platform every single night of the week. Wednesday game? There's an app for that. Thursday? A different app. Sunday comes with its usual pile of options plus whatever new service decided this was the year to get into football, and Monday night is now split across two networks like anyone was asking for that. It is, genuinely, a ridiculous number of ways to watch a fantasy lineup lose by 0.4 points. Use one of them. This is also year one of a brand-new three-year division cycle — the league shuffled the groupings this offseason, so a few of these early matchups are first-time rivalries. The first three weeks of the schedule are all division games, so early bragging rights are on the line before anyone even gets a look at the rest of the league. Here's how each division shapes up.

Shane Falco Division

The Blue Bloods

The most decorated group in the league on paper, and it shows in how these rosters are built. Nick's Get Griddy With It — the winningest franchise in league history (105-68 all-time), two titles, riding a three-year playoff streak — is stacked at every skill spot with Josh Allen, Derrick Henry, Justin Jefferson, and Drake London, with real bench depth behind them. Parker's Unsolicited Dak Pics runs its whole operation through Christian McCaffrey's workload, with George Pickens as the complementary piece, still chasing a first title after finishing runner-up. Justin's Fat JaMarcus Russell has a title in the bank from 2015 but missed the playoffs last year — the plan now hinges on two former first-round quarterbacks, Caleb Williams and Matthew Stafford, actually earning their reps. And Rebecca's The Hurts Locker is built the deepest top to bottom in the division; Jalen Hurts and Jonathan Taylor anchor a roster with almost no structural holes.

— Colin Audible
Staff Pick to Win Division: The Hurts Locker (Rebecca)

Bobbie Miles Division

Ring Row

This division has won three of the last four league championships, and honestly, it shows. Robby's Baker's Bunch has two of them (2022, 2024), Jordan's Jayden's Mambo No. 4s has the other (2023), and both walk in with the kind of quiet confidence that comes from actually knowing how to close. Jordan just keeps winning — two titles, a four-year playoff streak, the best record in the league over the last three years — with Lamar Jackson, Jayden Daniels, and Bijan Robinson leading the way. Robby's lived the full rollercoaster — champion in '22, bottom of the barrel the next year, champion again in '24, bottom again last year — and you get the sense he's due for another high. Matt's DAMNNN Daniel!! walks in with maybe the best receiver duo in the league in Puka Nacua and Ja'Marr Chase, and the lingering question of whether Travis Kelce has one more great year in him. And Charlie's BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY is still chasing that signature moment — Amon-Ra St. Brown and Terry McLaurin give him hope, but there's real nervous energy around the QB room.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Staff Pick to Win Division: Jayden's Mambo No. 4s (Jordan)

Paul Crewe Division

Contenders & Ghosts

The data here tells an interesting story. Borovay's Mahomes Depot is the reigning champion by outcome, not by process — he snuck into the playoffs and rode a hot streak to the title, which isn't exactly a repeatable model, especially with Mahomes's recovery timeline still an unknown variable. Suekoff's Breece Yourselves owns the league's longest active playoff drought at four years, a number that should be genuinely alarming if Jaxson Dart doesn't take a real step forward. Gembo's The Idea of BO-lieve posted the best point differential in the league last season and still didn't get past a runner-up finish — a textbook case of results lagging the underlying numbers, and a strong signal for positive regression this year. And Ross's Barking up the Wrong Tree has a concentrated top three (Prescott, Barkley, Brown) propping up a shallow supporting cast — a fragile model if any one of them slips.

— Ivana Blitz
Staff Pick to Win Division: The Idea of BO-lieve (Gembo)
Preseason Power Rankings
Ranked on full roster strength — top-end talent, depth, and season-long outlook.
  1. 1 ●
    Jayden's Mambo No. 4s (Jordan)
    Preseason tradition at this point. Four straight playoff appearances, the most wins in the league over that span, and a championship mixed in. Lamar Jackson and Jayden Daniels give him the league's best QB room, and having CJ Stroud and Malik Willis on the bench is borderline unfair. Bijan Robinson is a legitimate #1 overall fantasy RB, while Kenneth Walker should get every opportunity to thrive as KC's feature back. The WR room behind Nico Collins may be the only real hole. Until someone knocks Jordan off the mountain, he gets the top spot.
    2025: 11-3, 4th place
  2. 2 ●
    The Hurts Locker (Rebecca)
    Coming off her best season yet. Rebecca finished 3rd a year ago and brings back the core that got her there: Hurts, Jonathan Taylor, and Malik Nabers. Hurts and Herbert give her a ridiculous QB room, while a Breece Hall bounce-back could give her one of the nastiest 1-2 punches at RB. A healthy Nabers is elite, and Rome Odunze looks primed for a real Year 3 leap. There aren't many holes here.
    2025: 9-5, 3rd place
  3. 3 ●
    DAMNNN Daniel!! (Matt)
    Best WR duo in the league, full stop. Matt has made the playoffs in two of the last three seasons, and a return starts with Ja'Marr Chase and Puka Nacua — arguably the best 1-2 punch at WR anywhere. Purdy and Daniel Jones give him a solid, if unspectacular, QB room, and De'Von Achane looks like a monster-year candidate. Bucky Irving and David Montgomery add reliable RB depth. The big question: does Travis Kelce have one more elite season left?
    2025: 4-10, 10th place
  4. 4 ●
    Mahomes Depot (Borovay)
    Proving it wasn't a fluke starts now. The defending champ finally got the monkey off his back last year. Lamb and Bowers give him an absurd foundation, while Cook and Skattebo could form one of the league's best RB tandems, with Swift as a proven fallback option. The question is QB — Mahomes is coming off injury and hasn't been an elite fantasy scorer in a while, and Tyler Shough is a giant unknown. The roster is loaded; the QB room decides if this is a repeat or a reunion tour.
    2025: 7-7, 1st place
  5. 5 ●
    Get Griddy With It (Nick)
    The biggest weekly edge in the league. Josh Allen gives Nick perhaps the biggest QB advantage anywhere, and he's going to need it — the WR room (Jefferson, London, McMillan) all come with their own QB questions attached. The real swing factor is at RB: does Derrick Henry have one more elite season, and can Quinshon Judkins produce in what could be a rough Cleveland offense? If the backs hit, this is a championship roster.
    2025: 8-6, 6th place
  6. 6 ●
    Baker's Bunch (Robby)
    The league's ultimate pendulum. Champion in 2022, near the bottom in 2023, champion in 2024, near the bottom in 2025 — the pattern says he's due. Burrow staying upright, Jeanty getting the massive workload expected in Vegas, and DeVonta Smith taking advantage of AJ Brown's departure could make this roster dangerous. Zay Flowers keeps improving, and Jeremiyah Love has real breakout potential.
    2025: 4-10, 11th place
  7. 7 ●
    The Idea of BO-lieve (Gembo)
    Finally, a team worth believing in? Still the only original 2013 GM without a championship, and this might be the roster that finally gets him there. Jahmyr Gibbs could see a monster workload with a real shot at finishing as the #1 RB in fantasy. Drake Maye has more weapons, and Bo Nix is thriving in Sean Payton's offense. Chris Olave should be a target monster, but Jaylen Waddle's fit and rookie Carnell Tate add some uncertainty — and Luther Burden is a name to watch as a breakout candidate.
    2025: 10-4, 5th place
  8. 8 ●
    Unsolicited Dak Pics (Parker)
    A championship ceiling, buried in question marks. Coming off his best season ever, riding Christian McCaffrey to the championship game — and once again, everything may hinge on whether McCaffrey stays healthy. George Pickens would be a fantastic WR2, but can he carry the room as the top option? Can DJ Moore bounce back as Josh Allen's top target? Christian Watson remains pure boom-or-bust, and Kyler Murray is the wild card underneath it all.
    2025: 11-3, 2nd place
  9. 9 ●
    Breece Yourselves (Suekoff)
    The league's longest active playoff drought. Hasn't made the postseason since 2021, which makes this ranking a dangerous one to hang a hat on. Jaxson Dart has real breakout potential, the Mayfield-to-Egbuka connection could be special, and Tyler Warren might be the best TE in the league not named Bowers or McBride. Everything hinges on the RB room — Hampton, Tuten, Stevenson. If the young backs hit, #9 is going to look awfully low in hindsight.
    2025: 5-9, 9th place
  10. 10 ●
    Barking up the Wrong Tree (Ross)
    It all comes down to Saquon. Missed the playoffs two straight years, coming painfully close last season. Barkley was the RB1 in 2024 before falling to RB15 a year ago — Ross needs the 2024 version back to finally break through. Chase Brown is a name to like, Dak Prescott and Jared Goff give him a strong QB room, and AJ Brown could be in for a monster season. The problem is depth: enough firepower to make the playoffs, but the stars need to stay stars.
    2025: 6-8, 7th place
  11. 11 ●
    Fat JaMarcus Russell (Justin)
    A lot of "we'll see." At 9-19 over the last two seasons, Justin is in his longest playoff drought, dating back to 2023. He walked away from his two first-round picks with Caleb Williams and Matthew Stafford, and Tee Higgins, Travis Etienne, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba give him a strong returning core. There's real upside in Caleb and a potential Etienne bounce-back, but TreVeyon Henderson is already hurt, Father Time is undefeated against Stafford, and Marvin Harrison Jr. still hasn't become the star everyone expected.
    2025: 6-8, 8th place
  12. 12 ●
    BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY (Charlie)
    Working the waiver wire and hoping nobody notices. After finishing last in 2025, Charlie was supposed to be sitting at 1.01 — except he'd traded that pick away two years earlier. He clawed his way back into round 2 for MarShawn Lloyd, but the roster thins out fast after that. Kyren Williams and Amon-Ra St. Brown are legitimate stars, but Tony Pollard and Terry McLaurin will need to carry serious weight. The QB room (Cam Ward, Kirk Cousins) offers very little security, and the bench doesn't inspire much confidence.
    2025: 3-11, 12th place
Week 1 Matchups
This week's slate, covered by our three division beat writers: Colin Audible (Shane Falco), Frank Lee Hurtz (Bobbie Miles), and Ivana Blitz (Paul Crewe).

Barking up the Wrong Tree vs The Idea of BO-lieve

Run the numbers and the swing factor is obvious: Saquon Barkley. He was the RB1 in the entire league two years ago and fell all the way to RB15 last season — that's about as wide a variance band as any player in this league carries into Week 1. Chase Brown and AJ Brown round out a solid supporting cast, but Kraft and Brooks are both banged up and there's not much cushion behind them. Gembo's distribution is flatter across the board, so even a shaky performance from any one piece barely moves the total, and a Jadarian Price breakout out of the backfield only widens the gap. Concentration risk versus depth: one of the oldest storylines in projection modeling, and it usually ends the same way.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: The Idea of BO-lieve

Baker's Bunch vs Jayden's Mambo No. 4s

This is the kind of game you can feel building all week. Robby's been staring at the injury report on Jeremiyah Love like it's a horoscope, trying to read something into a 50/50 coin flip. Jordan's just... calm. Unbothered. That's what having Lamar Jackson and Bijan Robinson does to a guy who's made the playoffs four years running and has genuinely stopped being surprised by any of this. If Love plays, it's a real slugfest. If he doesn't, you can already feel the air go out of the room.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: Jayden's Mambo No. 4s

Unsolicited Dak Pics vs Fat JaMarcus Russell

Both teams walked into the draft with zero quarterbacks and walked out with two apiece in the first two picks — Justin on Caleb Williams and Matthew Stafford, Parker on Kyler Murray and Jordan Love. Same build, different foundation. Justin needs Caleb to clear a high bar here — not just serviceable, but a legitimate top-five fantasy quarterback. Funny wrinkle: Stafford was actually on Parker's roster last year and helped carry him to the championship game. Now he's lining up against him — does he have one more run left in the tank for someone else? Parker's plan is simpler on paper: keep McCaffrey on the field and let Murray play changeup if the ground game stalls. Whichever quarterback room holds up better wins this one.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Unsolicited Dak Pics

Breece Yourselves vs Mahomes Depot

Borovay's title defense opens with more question marks than answers. Mahomes is trending back from ACL/LCL surgery but hasn't graded out as an elite fantasy scorer in years even at full health, and the real hedge if he sits is Aaron Rodgers off the bench — not exactly a security blanket. What actually keeps this offense afloat is the talent around him: Cook, Skattebo, Lamb, and Bowers give it a real floor no matter who's throwing. Suekoff counters with a flatter, lower-variance build — Dart, Wilson, Egbuka, and Warren all contribute without needing one outcome to break right. Less star power, fewer landmines. In a coin-flip opener like this, that's usually the safer number to back.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: Breece Yourselves

The Hurts Locker vs Get Griddy With It

Neither offense has an obvious weakness to scheme around, which is rare this early in a season. Rebecca's group is fully healthy behind Jalen Hurts and Breece Hall, who's cleared that groin issue right on schedule, and there's no clear soft spot to attack on Nick's side either, with Josh Allen, Derrick Henry, and Justin Jefferson all at full strength. This is a game where the better game plan probably matters less than which team's alpha wins his individual matchup on Sunday. Sometimes there's no chess to play, just two good rosters lining up.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Get Griddy With It

DAMNNNN Daniel!!!! vs BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

You can already write the recap for this one. Matt's offense has main-character energy — Chase and Nacua both banged up but expected to play, which at this point is basically a formality. Charlie's rolling into Week 1 just hoping Cam Ward doesn't turn the ball over four times and praying MarShawn Lloyd finds a crease somewhere. This isn't really a rivalry, it's a mood. Circle it, laminate it, tell your grandkids where you were.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: DAMNNNN Daniel!!!!
