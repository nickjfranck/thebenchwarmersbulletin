Week 1 Recap

Week 1 Is In the Books, and Everyone's Still Undefeated in Their Own Head

By the League Newsroom · Week 1 Edition

Season 14 is officially underway. It's a new year, the record book is clean, and for one glorious week everybody got to believe their roster was the one that finally had it all figured out. Then the games actually happened.

Here's the thing about Week 1: the scoreboard doesn't always tell the real story. Some of this week's losers should feel better about themselves than some of this week's winners — a bad beat with a monster score on the bench is a different kind of loss than getting run off the field, and a win propped up by three turnovers from your own quarterback isn't exactly a statement. A few teams are walking away thinking this is finally their year. A few more are already bracing for a long one. By the numbers: Borovay's win came by the largest margin in the league, Nick and Rebecca played the closest game of the week, and Matt and Charlie combined for the lowest score on the slate. Here's how each division's matchups shook out, division by division, with our beat writers on the call.

Shane Falco Division

The Blue Bloods
Get Griddy With It Survives a High-Scoring Affair

Nick beat Rebecca, 132.98 to 129.28 — makes it 7-1 all-time in the sibling series. Sam Darnold left with a hip injury on the first possession, which is normally the kind of thing that ends a Sunday early. Josh Allen didn't let it. Four total touchdowns. Derrick Henry: 144 yards, three scores. Justin Jefferson: two touchdowns on 8 catches for 92 yards, which is his entire 2025 touchdown total in one afternoon. Not everyone showed up, though. Quinshon Judkins managed 33 yards. Drake London caught 2 passes for 29. The offense concerns from August didn't disappear just because the quarterback bailed them out. Fine performance overall. Moving on.

Rebecca's side wasn't bad either, for the record. Jalen Hurts threw three touchdown passes, Jonathan Taylor went for 120 total yards and two scores, Breece Hall added over 100 yards and a touchdown. Kyle Pitts caught nothing. No receiver cracked double digits. She still finished top-half in the league this week. Doesn't matter. The standings don't care how you got there. One more thing: Bryce Young threw for 361 yards and three touchdowns and ran in a fourth — third-most points by any quarterback in the league this week. He was on her bench. Final: Nick.

— Colin Audible
Justin Rides Caleb Williams to a Season-Opening Win

Justin wins his third straight opener. Caleb Williams: 265 yards and 2 touchdowns passing, 65 yards and 2 more on the ground. Kyle Monangai added 100 yards and a score in the same blowout. Jaxon Smith-Njigba: 8 catches, 122 yards, a touchdown, no complaints there. Matt Stafford was the soft spot — 155 yards, an interception — and needs to be better than that if the two-QB build is going to hold up past September. Fine win. Not going to pretend it was complicated.

Parker's night ended before it started. Kyler Murray took a hit, left the game, finished with negative points, and there's no real backup quarterback on the bench to cover for it. That's not bad luck. That's a roster-construction problem, and somebody should have caught it back in August. Jordan Love played well — 387 yards, 2 touchdowns, a pick. Christian Watson: 147 yards, 2 scores. Javonte Williams found the end zone twice. DJ Moore looked the part in his Bills debut. Good numbers. Wrong result. Fix the quarterback room and this doesn't happen again.

— Colin Audible

Bobbie Miles Division

Ring Row
Jayden's Mambo No. 4s Wins a Shootout for the Ages

Some games you watch. This one you experienced. Both sides emptied every ounce of firepower they had, and for one night the Bobbie Miles Division looked less like a fantasy league and more like a heavyweight card. Kenneth Walker introduced himself to his new team the loudest way possible — 173 rushing yards, a score on the ground, another through the air — while Bijan Robinson quietly, almost politely, racked up 173 total yards of his own. And then there was Lamar, doing what Lamar does: 324 yards, two touchdowns, the kind of performance that doesn't need an adjective because the name already is one. Jordan's fifth straight Week 1 win isn't luck anymore. It's a personality trait.

And somewhere in the wreckage, Robby is sitting with the third-highest score in the entire league and absolutely nothing to show for it. Ashton Jeanty looked like a bell cow — 147 total yards, two touchdowns. Zay Flowers turned into a touchdown-scoring missile — 150 yards on just 5 catches. Trevor Lawrence threw for 245 yards and four scores. Every piece of this roster showed up ready to win a football game, and it still wasn't the biggest story on the field. That's Week 1 for you: sometimes you do everything right, and someone else just does it louder.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
DAMNNN Daniel!! Snaps a Streak, Edges BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

There's a version of this story where nothing about it is surprising, and then there's what actually happened. Matt finally, finally gets his first Week 1 win since 2021 — and the poetry of it is that it came against the exact same opponent as last time. David Montgomery left Detroit behind this offseason and brought his touchdown radar with him to Houston, punching in two scores on the ground and adding one through the air like the zip code never mattered. Brock Purdy carved the Rams up for 205 yards and three touchdowns, and tucked away on the bench — unbothered, unused, and quietly sensational — Jalen Coker put up 8 catches for 138 yards and two touchdowns. Somebody go find out if that's a real thing now.

For Charlie, this one stings in a very specific way: it's his first Week 1 loss since 2021, and it came at the hands of the same man who beat him the last time it happened. He didn't go down easy, though. Isaiah Likely turned into an absolute problem — 8 catches, 78 yards, 2 touchdowns — and Amon-Ra St. Brown matched him stride for stride with 10 grabs, 67 yards, and 2 scores of his own. Kyren Williams found the end zone even on a quiet 65-yard night. It just wasn't the kind of quiet that wins football games.

— Frank Lee Hurtz

Paul Crewe Division

Contenders & Ghosts
The Idea of BO-lieve Holds Off Barking up the Wrong Tree

The numbers had this one right from kickoff, even if the final margin wasn't quite the laugher it looked like at halftime. Gembo gets his first Week 1 win since 2023 by 8 points, and the process behind it was about as clean as these things get. Jahmyr Gibbs did exactly what a workhorse back is supposed to do — 186 total yards, two touchdowns — and the receiving corps backed him up: Chris Olave turned 10 targets into 182 yards, Trey McBride added 9 catches for 95 yards and a score. Drake Maye threw three interceptions and the offense still found a way to win, which tells you everything about how top-heavy the efficiency was elsewhere.

Ross's numbers are a lot less fun to read. His first-round pick, AJ Brown, is now on IR with a six-week timeline — not exactly the return anyone projected in August. Ladd McConkey led the team with 82 yards and a touchdown and now he's day-to-day too, because of course he is. Saquon Barkley had a perfectly nice real-life football day — 15 carries, 83 yards — and a fantasy day so pedestrian it barely moved the needle. No touchdown, no impact. With his top two options compromised, Ross is staring down a depth chart that reads Cooper Kupp and Calvin Ridley as the next men up. Good luck with that.

— Ivana Blitz
Mahomes Depot Blows Out Breece Yourselves

Borovay banks his third straight Week 1 win by the largest margin in the league this week, and the box score reads like it was designed by committee. Patrick Mahomes made his return look routine — 184 yards, two touchdowns through the air, one more on the ground — the exact outcome the projections needed and didn't fully trust until it happened. D'Andre Swift did the actual heavy lifting on the ground — 124 yards & three touchdowns — in a Bears offense that put up 59 points because apparently that's just a Tuesday for them now. And then there's Tyler Shough, who threw for over 400 yards, three touchdowns, and also turned the ball over three separate times, because nothing in this league is ever allowed to be simple. Nobody's algorithm loves that kind of variance. Nobody's algorithm cares that it still won by that much.

Suekoff's day was tidier, which is another way of saying it was worse. Jaxson Dart was genuinely good — 230 yards, 3 touchdowns, 54 more on the ground — and then the offense around him simply declined to participate. Garrett Wilson led the receivers with a whopping 6 catches for 79 yards. Baker Mayfield threw zero touchdown passes and fumbled three times, which is its own kind of impressive. The real headline: Suekoff's second-highest scorer this week was his defense. That is not a compliment. That is a cry for help.

— Ivana Blitz

Week 2 Matchups
This week's slate, covered by our three division beat writers: Colin Audible (Shane Falco), Frank Lee Hurtz (Bobbie Miles), and Ivana Blitz (Paul Crewe).
Writer's Record: 4-2 in Week 1.

Breece Yourselves vs Barking up the Wrong Tree

Both teams are 0-1, and neither wants to see 0-2 next to their name. That's the whole stakes package here. Ross still hasn't solved his AJ Brown problem — Calvin Ridley managed zero catches last week, Cooper Kupp had two for 35 yards, and neither looks like an answer. He needs Saquon Barkley to actually find the end zone this time, not just pile up empty carries. Suekoff's counting on Baker Mayfield bouncing back against his old team's defense, which should lift Emeka Egbuka right along with him. Rhamondre Stevenson is clearly the workhorse now, and Omarion Hampton's role is the one worth tracking. Numbers lean Suekoff.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: Breece Yourselves

DAMNNN Daniel!! vs Baker's Bunch

Fantasy has a sense of humor sometimes, and this matchup is the punchline. Matt scored the third-fewest points in the league last week and won. Robby scored the third-most and lost. Everything about that feels due for a correction, and I think it happens this week. Chase and Nacua were quiet for Matt — that doesn't happen twice. If David Montgomery keeps finding the end zone and Achane and Irving get rolling behind him, this offense looks completely different. Robby's problem isn't that Jeanty, Flowers, and Lawrence can't repeat last week. It's that he might need all three to do it again, every single week, just to keep pace. He started 0-5 last year. He's on the verge of an 0-2 hole this year. If Jeremiyah Love starts taking real command of that Arizona backfield, it takes some of that pressure off Jeanty's back.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: DAMNNN Daniel!!

Get Griddy With It vs Unsolicited Dak Pics

Nick and Parker are brothers-in-law, for whatever that's worth once the whistle blows. This week it's worth a little extra: both are down a quarterback. Sam Darnold is out for Nick. Kyler Murray is still in concussion protocol for Parker — not a definite no, but if he can't go, Parker doesn't really have a plan for it. Nick's got the better answer regardless. Whether he keeps Quinshon Judkins in the lineup after a 33-yard week or swaps in Chuba Hubbard, who scored twice off the bench, is worth watching. Parker needs more than five combined catches for 32 yards out of Pickens and Ferguson. And it's a little funny that DJ Moore is Parker's receiver while Josh Allen — the guy throwing him the ball — plays for Nick. Moore's big games are Allen's big games. That's just how it works.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Get Griddy With It

Mahomes Depot vs The Idea of BO-lieve

The only matchup this week between two 1-0 teams, which makes it the closest thing to a real title fight the schedule has to offer. Borovay won last week with James Cook doing basically nothing, and while D'Andre Swift probably won't repeat three touchdowns, this backfield has three different backs who all know where the end zone is. I remain skeptical of Tyler Shough — he gets one more week to embarrass me — and did Mahomes actually tear his ACL last year? Sure didn't look like it. Gembo, meanwhile, won with both quarterbacks playing badly, and that math doesn't work twice. As long as Jahmyr Gibbs is on the field, though, Gembo stays in this.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: Mahomes Depot

Jayden's Mambo No. 4s vs BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

The highest-scoring team in the league against the lowest-scoring team in the league, and there isn't much drama in picking a side here. Jordan's the overwhelming favorite, as he should be. Nobody knows if Kenneth Walker repeats what he did last week, but pair him with Bijan, Lamar, and Jayden Daniels and it barely matters — that's an absurd amount of firepower even on an ordinary day. Charlie has real bright spots: Amon-Ra St. Brown, Kyren Williams, and an Isaiah Likely breakout nobody saw coming. It's just not enough. The quarterback room is thin, the depth is thinner, and there isn't enough juice here to keep pace.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: Jayden's Mambo No. 4s

The Hurts Locker vs Fat JaMarcus Russell

Rebecca lost the closest game of the week. Justin won comfortably and is chasing his first 2-0 start since 2018. Justin's whole case depends on whether Caleb Williams can be even half of what he was last week — do that, and he's a legitimate elite fantasy quarterback instead of a boom-or-bust lottery ticket. Kyle Monangai's role is worth tracking. Matthew Stafford needs to be more involved. Marvin Harrison Jr. is still waiting on his moment. Rebecca's top three — Hurts, Taylor, Hall — did their job last week. The receivers didn't. Malik Nabers and Rome Odunze need to show up, and Kyle Pitts needs to catch literally one pass.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: The Hurts Locker
