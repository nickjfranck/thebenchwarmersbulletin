Editor's Note
The commissioner's office is floating a rule change, and this paper believes it deserves a fair hearing: league median scoring. Each week you'd still play your head-to-head opponent. But your score would also be measured against the middle score of the entire league. Land above the median and you bank a second win. Land below it and you pick up a second loss. Every team would finish every week at 2-0, 1-1 or 0-2.
The case for it: fewer seasons wrecked by running into the week's highest-scoring opponent, standings that reward the teams that actually score every week, and a reason to keep rooting for your players even after your matchup is lost. This isn't hypothetical, either. This week the two highest-scoring teams in the league played each other, so one of them was guaranteed a loss. Robby had the third-highest score in Week 1 and took a loss. Gembo finished top six in Week 2 and took a loss. Nick finished top six in Week 3 and took a loss. Sleeper and Yahoo both support it natively. Think it over, form an opinion, and bring it to the group chat, where it will be settled the way all league business is: loudly.
— Staff
Week 4 Recap

Half the League Sits at 1-3 as Two Unbeaten Teams Pull Away

By the League Newsroom · Week 4 Edition

Four weeks in, the standings have split into two very different leagues. Half of it sits at 1-3: Parker, Rebecca, Matt, Gembo, Suekoff, and Ross, all stuck on the same record for very different reasons. At the other end, only Borovay and Justin are still unbeaten, and Borovay's three-game lead in the Paul Crewe Division is already starting to look like a gap. In between, Nick, Robby, Jordan, and Charlie are fighting to stay on the right side of the line. This is the point of the season where managers find out who should and shouldn't be in their lineups, and the answers are getting harder to ignore.

A month of data is enough to sort the player pool. There are the roller coasters, like Jeremiyah Love, who keeps alternating big weeks with quiet ones. There are the consistent ballers, like Kenneth Walker, who still hasn't scored under 20 points all season. And the disappointments are starting to stick out, from Saquon Barkley's touchdown drought to George Pickens' missing breakout. Some draft-day regrets are surfacing too, with second-round picks like MarShawn Lloyd losing carries every week. Add in the bumps and bruises, with Lamar Jackson, Ja'Marr Chase, and Barkley all leaving games early, and the next few weeks could reshape the league. Full breakdowns below.

Get Griddy With It Wins a Barnburner Over Jayden's Mambo No. 4s

Game of the week, and it delivered. The two highest-scoring teams in the league met, and one of them was always going to walk away with a loss. Nick made sure it wasn't him. Chuba Hubbard handled 20 carries and finished with 2 touchdowns. Tetairoa McMillan turned in a career day, 14 catches and 2 scores. Josh Allen found the end zone twice without breaking a sweat, and Will Reichard kicked 5 field goals, which is a lot of offense for a guy who only shows up for the last 40 yards. Nick moves to 3-1 and gets the better of a heavyweight bout. Final: Nick.

Jordan hit a number that beats every other team in the league, just not the one he was playing. Kenneth Walker and Bijan Robinson combined for 4 touchdowns and over 300 rushing yards. Nico Collins returned and added 2 scores of his own. It wasn't enough. Then came the real damage: Lamar Jackson left with an ankle injury, and Jordan has already lost Jayden Daniels. One more hit at quarterback and the season gets complicated.

— Colin Audible
Baker's Bunch Cruises Past Barking up the Wrong Tree Behind a Big Day From Burrow

Three straight wins, a one-game lead in the Bobbie Miles Division, and a ceiling game that explains why Robby keeps showing up near the top of every list. Joe Burrow turned the ball over three times and still posted his best fantasy score of the season, because the one thing that matters in this league is points, not manners. Zay Flowers and Deebo Samuel both found the end zone, and Ashton Jeanty's workload held steady. The one soft spot is a familiar one. Jeremiyah Love went quiet again, but the data says his rollercoaster is due for another climb, and Robby has the schedule to prove he can survive the dips.

Ross, meanwhile, is living the same story, and the numbers keep telling it. Dak Prescott and Jared Goff did their jobs at quarterback, and the rest of the roster treated the afternoon like an optional meeting. Saquon Barkley left with a hamstring injury, Ladd McConkey is nursing a rib, and the one real flicker of light stings the most: Emanuel Wilson ran for 120 yards and two touchdowns while sitting on Ross's bench. Still the lowest-scoring team in the league, and the box score has the receipts.

— Ivana Blitz
Fat JaMarcus Russell Gets Past DAMNNN Daniel!! and Stays Perfect

Justin's perfect season rolls on at 4-0, and this one had a little of everything. Kyle Monangai ran for 146 yards and 2 touchdowns, Tee Higgins turned in his best game of the season, and George Kittle found the end zone for a third straight week. Even Jaxon Smith-Njigba's quietest day of the year still came with 76 yards. The quarterbacks did just enough, and the supporting cast did the rest.

Three losses in a row for Matt, and the cruelest part is that the pieces were there. Puka Nacua returned and made it look casual, and Brock Purdy tossed two touchdowns, but those two, along with the kicker, were the only players to reach double digits. Then came the sucker punch: Ja'Marr Chase walked off with a concussion. Travis Kelce was bottled up, Daniel Jones was rough again, and a team can't win a week with three contributors.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
The Hurts Locker Beats Breece Yourselves for Its First Win of the Season

Rebecca's letter to the editor argued that her team had allowed the most points in the league through three weeks, which sounds like an excuse right up until the box score agrees with it. This week, it did. The win came against the week's lowest-scoring team, sure, but it also came on the back of three separate 21-plus-point performances, which is not an accident. Jonathan Taylor ran for 95 yards and a pair of scores, Bryce Young threw for 329 and 2 touchdowns, and Malik Nabers finally had his best game of the year. The one concerning data point is Dalton Kincaid, who has just 3 catches in the two weeks since his huge start. Still, one win beats zero, and the data suggests the points-allowed luck may finally be turning.

Suekoff slips to 1-3 with the fewest points in the league this week, and the lineup tells the story. The injury bug that already took Jaxson Dart and Baker Mayfield claimed his fill-in, Marcus Mariota, too. Nobody in the starting lineup topped 15 points, while three players on the bench did. Ollie Gordon (100 rushing yards and a score) and Romeo Doubs (2 touchdowns) did the real work from the pine. Not great, Suekoff, but at least the bench is carrying its weight.

— Ivana Blitz
BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY Edges The Idea of BO-lieve in the Week's Closest Game

Charlie climbs back to .500 in the tightest matchup of the week, and the road there ran through Kyren Williams, who did it by land and by air with 2 rushing touchdowns and 10 catches. Kirk Cousins' run of three touchdown passes a game finally ended, and he still turned in his best fantasy day of the year, a reminder that streaks are only numbers and production is real. Cam Ward added a score, and Tony Pollard hit pay dirt for the first time this season. The margin was thin enough to count on one hand, and the one lingering question sits at the back of the roster: Charlie still lacks a reliable flex.

A third straight loss for Gembo, and the trouble is a team that can't get everything working in the same week. Drake Maye finally remembered how to play football, with 3 touchdowns, while Bo Nix was Bo Nix, average to the last. Jahmyr Gibbs, the 30-point merchant, finally had his worst game of the year, and the second running back spot remains a black hole. Losing is one thing. Losing the closest game of the week is its own special kind of cruel.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Mahomes Depot Takes Down Unsolicited Dak Pics and Opens a Three-Game Division Lead

Borovay stays undefeated, and four weeks in, the Paul Crewe Division is starting to look like a formality, a three-game lead over everyone else. CeeDee Lamb had a season-best day, 17 catches and a touchdown, and the knockout punch came from a receiver on Parker's favorite team. Brock Bowers has been on top of his game since missing the first two weeks. Mahomes wasn't perfect, but 225 yards and a pair of touchdowns was plenty. James Cook found the end zone for a third straight game. Final: Borovay.

A week after getting his first win, Parker is back in the loss column. DJ Moore got hurt again early and left without contributing much of anything, and George Pickens played second fiddle to Lamb all game. Kyler Murray's completion percentage went up, but he never found the end zone. The one bright spot: Javonte Williams hit pay dirt three times. It just wasn't enough.

— Colin Audible

Week 5 Matchups
Week 5 brings the first bye weeks, and with them the first real test of depth. Two matchups pit 1-3 teams against each other, and a couple of seasons could end on Sunday. One more features two of the best teams in the league.
Writer's Record: 11-13 on the season (3-3 in Week 4).

Get Griddy With It vs Breece Yourselves

Top scorer of the week against the bottom of the barrel. Jefferson's status is uncertain, and even if he plays, he's catching passes from Kyler. Nick doesn't flinch. Allen, Henry and London give him plenty of firepower, and he can afford a couple of guys to have an off day. Suekoff can't. He needs everything to click, and he's coming off his worst performance of the year. Ollie Gordon is the only thing to build on, but a bright spot is not an offense.

Bye Week Players: Nick: Chuba Hubbard, Tetairoa McMillan.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: Get Griddy With It

Jayden's Mambo No. 4s vs Fat JaMarcus Russell

Ask a hundred managers how this one ends and you'll get a hundred shrugs. Jordan's fate rides on the ankle of Lamar Jackson and the status of Jayden Daniels, both questionable. He'll also be without Kenneth Walker, the top-ranked running back in the league, who is on a bye. Justin, meanwhile, is still without Caleb Williams, but his quarterbacks have held it together so far. If either of Jordan's quarterbacks plays, this leans Jordan. If neither does, Justin is likely to keep his perfect season alive. The stars on each side are the ones to watch, Bijan Robinson against Jaxon Smith-Njigba, a pair of players who can win a week by themselves. Either one can hit for 40-plus. Whoever gets there first probably decides it.

Bye Week Players: Jordan: Kenneth Walker.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: Fat JaMarcus Russell

Baker's Bunch vs Mahomes Depot

The matchup of the week, and the league is paying attention. Robby has won three in a row. Borovay hasn't lost. Something has to give, and the thing to watch is depth. Mahomes is on a bye, so the champ has to win without the one player who has kept the engine running. Robby, meanwhile, needs Jeremiyah Love to find his footing, ankle willing. Two teams built to win, one reason to doubt, and the data says this one is closer than the records suggest.

Bye Week Players: Borovay: Patrick Mahomes.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: Baker's Bunch

The Hurts Locker vs DAMNNN Daniel!!

The first battle of the 1-3s, and the loser is in real trouble. Rebecca just got her first win, and the offense finally looked alive. Jonathan Taylor is carrying the load, and Breece Hall might even return from his quad injury. Matt is in a free fall with three straight losses, a banged-up roster and a big question about Ja'Marr Chase's concussion. Does it matter? Matt's offense is searching for answers, and one player won't supply them. One team saves its season. The other might be done.

Bye Week Players: Rebecca: Bryce Young. Matt: Travis Kelce.

— Colin Audible
Writer's Pick: The Hurts Locker

Barking up the Wrong Tree vs BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

Some teams can't catch a break, and Ross is currently the poster child. A long list of injuries has turned his roster into the lowest-scoring in the league, and this week brings more of the same. Saquon Barkley, still searching for his first touchdown, and Ladd McConkey, who hasn't scored since Week 1, are both questionable, which stretches a thin bench even thinner. Across the field, Charlie has been far from perfect, but he owns two players who can keep him in any matchup: Kyren Williams and Amon-Ra St. Brown. Kirk Cousins' recent form has to have him smiling, too. A little work around the edges is still needed, but he's in the better position by a wide margin.

Bye Week Players: Ross: Darren Waller.

— Frank Lee Hurtz
Writer's Pick: BRICKED4BRANDONAUBREY

The Idea of BO-lieve vs Unsolicited Dak Pics

The second battle of the 1-3s, and the numbers say it's a coin flip. Gembo has lost three straight. Parker has one win to his name. Seventh and eighth in the league in total points, separated by about five points. Elite running backs on both sides (Gibbs for Gembo, McCaffrey and Javonte Williams for Parker), strong receivers, and two of the better tight ends in the league. Every position lines up. The tiebreaker is the one spot both teams keep tripping over: quarterback. Maye and Nix against Murray and Love, and the highs have been rare. Somebody is going to have a big game, and the data says it's whichever one stops throwing it away.

— Ivana Blitz
Writer's Pick: The Idea of BO-lieve
